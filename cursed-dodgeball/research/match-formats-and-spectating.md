## 1. Illegal Soccer (Repotted, May 2026)

- 8-player servers, 4v4 Red vs Blue. A full server means everyone is playing.
- Casual matches form in-server: walk onto a Join Red or Join Blue pad in the lobby. Matches run on a timer, most matches 4 to 6 minutes. A team down by one at the buzzer plays on until they lose possession; equalise and it goes to overtime.
- Between matches: lobby intermission, item shop rolls (1,000 coins a roll), a practice area with an AI keeper. Coins pay out at the end of a match based on result.
- Ranked is a separate build: unlocked by owning 5 items, 10 placement matches, two ladders (Illegal with items, Legal without), global queue. Each team votes to ban one item from the other team. Repeated leaving gives a temporary ranked restriction; disconnects can rejoin.
- Replays and Rewards update (Sept 2026): every goal triggers a cinematic instant replay with no loading break. Lobby leaderboards, daily rewards. Clubs (squads) added Sept 26.
- Peak about 74k concurrent (Sept 2026).
- Spectating: [unverified] no source describes stands or a spectator camera. With an 8 cap and 4v4, the only watchers are late joiners. The watchable asset is the goal replay and clip culture, not a spectator mode.

Takeaway: one pitch, one tiny server, everyone plays. The watchable moment is engineered (replays), not a mode.

## 2. Huss Valley (Aug 31, 2026)

- Asymmetric tag: one random Chaser versus Runners in a small rectangular arena with lines at each end.
- Runners win by crossing 3 times with nobody caught. A caught Runner converts into a Chaser, so nobody sits out mid-round. Rescue Kits are single-use revives.
- Players wait in a lobby between matches with an explicit Spectate button to watch the current match.
- Server size conflicting: 16 (mmorocks) versus up to 30 (games.gg). [unverified which is current]
- Gems and coins to both sides at match end.

Takeaway: conversion instead of elimination, and one-button spectate from the lobby.

## 5. Real dodgeball tournament formats

- WDBF: 6v6, 6 balls, 40-minute match of two 20-minute halves, play as many sets as possible, 1 point per set, max 3 minutes per set, set ends on a wipe, sudden death at the end of each half. World Championship: round robin pools then knockout.
- Elite Dodgeball (US club): matches best of 3, every division runs round robin then double elimination.
- NCDA college: 15v15, 10 balls, two 25-minute halves, a point per full team elimination.
- School and rec: best of 3 with 3 to 5 minute sets; overtime is sudden death where the first player out loses; pools then top teams to a bracket.
- King's Court: multiple courts ranked, win moves you up a court, lose moves you down, all games start together, continuous ladder with no bracket.

Translation: a WDBF set (3 minutes max, ends on wipe, sudden death at the buzzer) is almost exactly a 60 to 120 second round. The real-sport unit is "play N sets inside a fixed clock, most sets wins", which maps to best-of-5 rounds in a 6 to 10 minute match.

## 6. Roblox DevForum and docs best practice

- Server size: set it to the minimum the design tolerates (typically 6 to 15) and prioritise full servers. A 50-cap server at 40 looks dead on the browse page and the algorithm reads low fill as low interest.
- Roblox Custom Matchmaking lets you weight signals or define your own from server attributes, so you can bias fill toward a server about to start a round without cross-server code.
- Cross-server matchmaking: MemoryStore queue plus ReserveServer and TeleportAsync to a match place. MemoryStore quota is 1,000 + 100 x concurrent users requests per minute per experience. Reserved server codes never expire. Wrap teleports in pcall with backoff, listen for TeleportInitFailed, batch all players into one TeleportAsync call.
- Spectator camera: set CameraSubject to the target's Humanoid, cycle a filtered list of alive players, validate on each switch, reset on round end. Common bugs are stale lists when the spectated player dies or leaves.
- Keeping waiters engaged: spectating alone is not enough; add a small practice or fight zone, and let players opt in or out of the next match so lobby sitters do not block starts. Intermissions of 10 to 20 seconds, rounds rarely over 5 minutes.
- Pure minigame loops stopped dominating after about 2017 because players feel no ownership; persistent stakes (rank, club, streak) fix this.
- Retention: one dev's day-1 retention problem was the first 1 to 3 minutes (onboarding and matchmaking), not the core loop.

## 7. Format comparison for a 12 to 16 player server, 4v4, 60 to 120 s rounds

| Format | Time to first play | Spectator experience | Dropouts | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Drop-in rounds (Illegal Soccer, Blade Ball) | One round or less | Late joiners auto-spectate with a timer; at 8 cap nobody watches | Trivial, replaced next round | Fastest loop, simplest tech, fills servers | No stakes beyond the round, bench has nothing to do |
| B. King of the court (winner stays) | Up to bench depth x round length, under 4 min at 16 | Excellent: the bench plays the winner next so they scout | Never blocks | Continuous, streak drama, invested watchers | Streaks can snowball, so cap at N wins |
| C. Single-elim bracket | Wait for 16 then your slot | Losers watch the final, then nothing | Worst: a leaver kills a match | Clear climax, prestige | Hard to fill, brittle, needs cross-server queue |
| D. Round robin | Waits for all teams | Little drama until the last game | Forfeits skew standings | Fair, lots of play | Long, weak climax, confusing for kids |
| E. Shrinking show (Fall Guys, DTI Style Showdown) | Wait for the show to fill | Eliminated spectate or leave | Already eliminated, no harm | Strong climax, very watchable | 15 to 20 minute commitment, early-out churn |

## 8. Recommendations

1. Ship drop-in rounds in a 12-player server with king-of-the-court rotation on top. Three teams of four: two play, one sits in the stands for at most two minutes and comes in as the challenger. Every round has an invested live audience and servers look full. Cap champion streaks at 3 wins with a crown cosmetic, then forced rotation. Intermission 15 to 20 seconds with a next-round timer.
2. Make spectating participatory. Auto-spectate anyone not in the round, a stands area facing the court, one-click free cam. Let watchers vote which cursed ball enters the next round or pick MVP. Cinematic replay on every elimination and last-player moment, which is the real watchable asset in Illegal Soccer and feeds clip culture.
3. Do not launch with a single-elim bracket as the primary mode. If you want tournament stakes, use a loss-capped win ladder (win 5 rounds before 3 losses this session) inside the drop-in loop. Zero extra infrastructure, nobody stranded.
4. Add a scheduled Show mode later: 16 players, 4 teams, both pairs play round one, losers become cursed spectators who can throw a cursed ball in from the stands during the final, whole show 12 to 18 minutes, on a timer per server or weekend events.
5. Numbers from real dodgeball: a round ends on wipe with a 90 to 120 s cap and sudden death at the buzzer (count remaining players, tied means next hit wins). A match is best of 5 rounds, 4 to 8 minutes. Ranked later: 10 placements, Elo per queue, a ban vote on one cursed ball per team, leaver lock, disconnect rejoin grace.
6. Tech implied: v1 is a single place, 12 cap, server-local round manager, auto-spectate camera, stands and replay cam, Custom Matchmaking signals to prefer servers in intermission. v2 adds MemoryStore queue, reserved match places, a permanent lobby hub, instant requeue.
